About

How can we guide tissue repair toward functional regeneration?

I am a biomedical engineer and postdoctoral associate at the University of Pittsburgh. My research brings together immunoengineering, biomaterials, and tissue mechanics to understand how cells interpret injury and how those responses shape healing. I have developed regenerative immunotherapies and organotypic culture platforms to manipulate these processes and follow remodeling over time.

My NHLBI K99/R00 research uses tissue-engineered vascular grafts to investigate how diabetic inflammation and mechanical cues influence host remodeling. My long-term goal is to identify controllable biological and biomechanical regulators of tissue fate, then use those mechanisms to design therapies that restore durable tissue function.

Research interests

Repair-fate biology, immune-stromal crosstalk, biomaterial remodeling, vascular repair, fibrosis, diabetes-impaired healing, and mechanics-guided regeneration.

Current support & training

NIH/NHLBI K99/R00 Pathway to Independence Award, 2026–2031. PhD in Biomedical Engineering, Johns Hopkins University, 2022.

Technical expertise

Longitudinal multiphoton imaging, quantitative 3D image analysis, biomechanical testing, electrospun biomaterials, organotypic culture, flow cytometry, and molecular profiling.

Research pillars

Mechanisms that guide repair fate

I

Repair-fate biology

Why do similar immune programs support regeneration in one injury context but drive fibrosis, degeneration, or failed repair in another?

II

Immunomechanics of biomaterial remodeling

How do material architecture, mechanical loading, inflammatory state, and stromal remodeling interact after implantation?

III

Predictive repair platforms

How can organotypic culture, longitudinal imaging, and mechanical measurements reveal when remodeling is progressing toward function or failure?
